# Known Limitations

## jades-hta: Utilities and disutilities

Both the discrete-event simulation (`UsesParameters.forceUtilityParameterValue`) and the decision tree generator (`PayoffWrapper.getQALEFormula`) apply the same rule: an absolute utility `u` (a `Utility` parameter with `isDisutility = false`) of a progression, the disease or an intervention is equivalent to a disutility of `(base utility of the population - u)`. If it is the only one active, the patient gets exactly `u`. The converted value is combined with the rest of disutilities by using the combination method of the experiment (`hasDisutilityCombinationMethod`), both for annual and one-time utilities.

This approach has the following limitations:

- **Base utility of the source.** Absolute utilities are converted into disutilities by subtracting them from the base utility of the model. If the source measured the utility against a different base utility, the decrement attributed to the condition will be wrong.
- **Single combination method.** Absolute utilities and disutilities are combined with a single method:
  - With `ADD`, an absolute utility measured in patients that already includes the effect of another condition is counted twice.
  - With `MAX`, independent disutilities are lost.
- **`MAX` in decision trees.** The combination method only applies to annual disutilities (one-time disutilities are always added). In the decision trees, `MAX` is applied to the discounted lifetime totals of each annual disutility, not year by year. If the disutilities have different onset or end ages, the result is not the maximum in each period (it should be computed by intervals).

### Possible improvements

- Treat each type differently: absolute utilities replace the current utility (taking the worst one), whereas disutilities are added.
- Add a multiplicative combination method.
- Indicate in OSDi the reference base utility of each source.

## jades-hta: Progressions without risk

A development or chronic manifestation without risk characterization (`hasRiskCharacterization`) that does not belong to a group of two or more alternative progressions (i.e., not affected by any combination rule, affected by a `CoexistentCombinationRule`, or by an `AlternativeCombinationRule` with a single member) is always active in its context. The decision tree generator (`DiseaseSubTreeGenerator`) does not create a chance node for it: its node is the only branch of its parent, with probability 1, so its payoffs apply to every leaf of the sub-tree that hangs from it. A manifestation without risk that depends on a development (`hasSubProgression`) is only active in the branches where such development is active. Within a group of two or more alternative progressions, the progression without risk takes the remaining probability (1 minus the sum of the rest); at most one progression of the group may lack a risk, and only if the group has no null progression.

The discrete-event simulation should interpret progressions without risk in the same way, but it currently does not: `DiseaseProgressionRiskFactory.getPathwayInstances` creates no pathway for a progression without risk, so it never happens, unless the population assigns it an initial proportion (`hasInitialProportion`). `DiseaseFactory` logs a warning for each manifestation that will never occur (see the conformance table below).

## jades-hta: Acute manifestations in decision trees

In a decision tree, an acute manifestation represents a **single episode** between the initial age of the population and death (the undiscounted life years of the leaf), or, if it defines a frequency, its repeated episodes. `DiseaseSubTreeGenerator` builds its node as for a chronic manifestation, so it can belong to coexistent or alternative combination rules, depend on a development, and carry effects (instant death, life expectancy reduction, increased mortality rate) that apply to the leaf:

- **Risk:** its risk characterization is the probability that the episode happens. Intervention effects on it follow the general rules (see "Effect magnitudes"). An acute manifestation without risk follows the rule of progressions without risk: the episode always happens in its context (e.g., a tree that starts in a patient suffering a migraine attack).
- **Frequency (`hasFrequency`, episodes per year):** it does not change the structure of the tree. The risk is the probability that the patient suffers the episodes (without risk, they always happen in their context, P56). The one-time payoffs whose owner is the node of the acute manifestation (its own, those of its strategies, and those of their outcomes and adverse events) are multiplied by the expected number of episodes: each one-time payoff x becomes an annual payoff f·x within the time window of the node (from its onset to the minimum of its end and the life years of the leaf, which are limited by the time horizon), and it is still always added, as a one-time payoff (P30, P55). The effects of the acute manifestation (instant death, life expectancy reduction) are not multiplied. If the acute manifestation is the target of the interventions, the tree starts with an episode and its frequency is ignored with a warning (to be reanalyzed). The frequency is not converted into the probability of at least one episode, 1 − e^(−f·H), which would discard the expected number of episodes.
- **One-time costs and utilities:** they apply once, when the episode happens: at the start of the model, or at the onset age if defined (see "Onset and end ages in decision trees"). A one-time utility is a QALY loss. Several one-time payoffs of the same acute manifestation are added.
- **Annual costs and utilities:** ignored with a warning (`DiseaseSubTreeGenerator.generateNode`), as in the discrete-event simulation. Before acute manifestations were rejected, they were treated as chronic ones, so their annual payoffs were applied from their onset until death.
- `hasDuration` is ignored, as for any other progression.

Tests: P28 (risk and one-time payoffs), P29 (annual payoffs ignored), P30 (risk and frequency), P31 (without risk), P32 (intervention effect on the risk), P55 (frequency with strategies and adverse events, and a time horizon) and P56 (only a frequency).

Divergences with the discrete-event simulation: the simulation counts every episode (an acute manifestation can happen again, according to its risk), whereas the tree counts one or, with a frequency, the expected number of episodes; and, if an acute manifestation defines several one-time costs or utilities, the simulation only uses the first one (with a warning), whereas the tree adds all of them. The tree does not model repeated episodes as a structure (D9 in `docs/osdi-1.3-diseno.md`): the frequency multiplies the payoffs.

## jades-hta: Target of the interventions

Every intervention declares its target (decision D1 of `docs/osdi-1.3-diseno.md`): the health condition to which it is applied, either the disease or an element of its progression (`isInterventionOf`, or its inverse `hasIntervention`). Time 0 is the moment when the intervention is applied: if the target is an element T, the model starts with the patients that present T.

`InterventionTargets.resolve` (shared by decision trees and the DES) reads the target of each intervention of the model in both directions, since the reasoner does not infer inverse properties. The ontology states that every intervention has exactly one target, but the same intervention individual may be shared by several models (as in the test ontologies), so only the targets included in the model are considered:

- An intervention without target in the model is assumed to target the disease, with a warning.
- An intervention with more than one target in the model (N15), a target that is neither the disease of the model nor one of its progressions, and compared interventions with different targets (N14) are rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`.
- In decision trees (`DiseaseSubTreeGenerator.resolveInterventionTarget`), stages and manifestations that depend on a development (N16) are rejected as targets with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`: a patient that presents a manifestation of a development also presents the development, but the manifestation may depend on several developments.

If the target is the disease, the tree is the same as before. If it is a clinical progression element T, the tree starts with the patients that present T (`DiseaseSubTreeGenerator.generateTargetSubTree`):

- The node of T is the only branch of the disease node, with probability 1. The risk of T and the combination rules that affect it are ignored.
- The payoffs and effects of the disease still apply, except for its instant death, which happens at the onset of the disease.
- Only the progressions that depend on T are included: the own manifestations of a development (I04), with their combination rules; nothing else for a manifestation (I03).
- The payoffs of the intervention are computed as before, from the start of the tree. If T is an acute manifestation, the annual costs and utilities of the intervention are ignored with a warning (`SimpleInterventionGenerator.generate`), as those of the acute manifestation itself.
- The onset and end ages of T keep the usual rules. TODO: this contradicts that every patient presents T at time 0; the DES rejects a target with a temporal constraint.

Newborn screening models (`NBSModel`) only support the disease as target.

In the DES (`DESInterventionTarget`), the intervention is active during the whole simulation, and the population describes the patients when the intervention is applied (time 0):

- If the target is the disease, nothing changes.
- If the target is a chronic manifestation T, every patient starts with T: its onset is not simulated and its risk is ignored, its one-time payoffs apply at time 0 (as in decision trees), its annual payoffs from time 0, and the progressions from T are planned from time 0, as if T had just appeared (D04). The other progressions present at time 0 are only those with an initial proportion declared by the population: JaDES does not deduce the progressions that precede T, and warns if every pathway to T requires a progression without a declared initial proportion.
- If the target is an acute manifestation T, every patient suffers an episode at time 0 (`TargetEpisodePathways`), and the next ones come from the frequency of T (`hasFrequency`, an exponential time between episodes with that rate; D07) or, if it has none, from its risk (D06); if T defines both, its risk is ignored with a warning. The effects of the intervention on the risk or the frequency act from the start. The one-time payoffs of the intervention apply with each episode, including the one at time 0 (`OSDiIntervention.appliesOneTimePayoffsOn`), and its annual payoffs are ignored with a warning. The one-time payoffs of the treatment and follow-up strategies of T in the arm also apply with each episode (D10).
- The population cannot contradict the target: an explicit initial proportion of T different from 1 (D05), a prevalence of the disease lower than 1, an incidence of the disease, or a declared initial progression that supersedes T are rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`. If the population does not declare the initial proportion of T, it is assumed to be 1 with a warning (D04).
- Developments (D08), stages, manifestations that depend on a development and targets with a temporal constraint (onset or end age) are rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`.
- If the interventions target the disease or a chronic manifestation, the one-time payoffs of a therapeutic intervention apply once, at time 0 (D03).

## jades-hta: Strategies and guidelines in decision trees

Interventions do not declare strategies (decision D2 of `docs/osdi-1.3-diseno.md`): an intervention with `hasStrategy` is rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`. An intervention changes the strategies of a health condition through a `StrategyReplacementEffect`, which affects exactly one health condition (`affects`) and defines exactly one replacement strategy or group of strategies (`hasReplacementStrategy`). Its magnitude, if any, is ignored with a warning.

**Replacement** (`SimpleInterventionGenerator.getStrategyReplacements`, `DiseaseSubTreeGenerator.getArmStrategies`): it applies in the arm of the intervention, wherever the affected health condition generates a node, and by type of strategy (detection, i.e. screening or diagnosis; follow-up; treatment, i.e. line of therapy). Each replacement strategy replaces the strategies of its type that the health condition declares, or it is assigned to the health condition if it had none of that type; the strategies of other types are kept (P34, P35). If the replacement is a group, each member replaces the strategies of its type (P41). Rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`: two strategy replacements of the same health condition in the same arm (N17), an effect of another class that affects a health condition (N18), and a strategy replacement declared by the disease or a progression (only interventions replace strategies).

**Costs and utilities** (`BranchDestinationNode.updateCosts`, `updateUtility`): the costs and utilities of the follow-up and treatment strategies of an element are added to its own costs and utilities (P33). Before, they were only used if the element had no own costs or utilities. A modeler may define an overall estimated cost for an element and a detailed cost through its strategies, and include in each model the one that suits it: only the items included in the model are read, so there is no ambiguity.

**Cost of a strategy** (`StrategyCosts`): its own costs (annual or one-time, as any other cost), plus the costs of the health technologies it uses (`usesHealthTechnology`). The guideline that applies to a technology is its own (`hasGuideline`) or, if it has none, that of the strategy (P40). Without guideline, the costs of the technology are ordinary payoffs (P36, T2). With a guideline, the cost c of the technology is a cost per application, and `appliesOneTime` is ignored (`PayoffWrapper.getGuidelineCostFormula`). With r = `hasFrequency` × 8760 / `hasPeriod` applications per year (the period is in hours; without period, the frequency is already annual):

- The guideline starts at the start of the node (MAX(0, onset age − initial age), or 0) plus `hasStartOffset` (in years), and it lasts L = MIN(`hasDuration`, `hasCount` / r), ignoring the missing terms.
- With a frequency: annual payoff r·c from the start to MIN(end of the node, start + L), where the end of the node is MIN(LY, end age − initial age), or LY (P36, P38, P39).
- Without a frequency: N·c once at the start, where N = `hasCount` (1 by default), only if the start is not after the end of the node (P37, P40). A period or a duration without frequency is ignored with a warning.
- With `ADD_DISTINCT`, a guideline cost shared by several nodes of a path (same cost and guideline) is counted once.

Limitations:

- TODO: guidelines with sub-guidelines (`hasSubGuideline`), condition expressions (`hasConditionExpression`) or application ages (`hasMinApplicationAge`, `hasMaxApplicationAge`) are rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException` (N19), since they change the number of applications and JaDES does not support them yet (neither the tree nor the DES read guidelines from OSDi). A strategy or technology with several guidelines is also rejected.
- `hasDose` is not used (the cost is per application). `followedByStrategy` (with a warning), the condition expressions of a strategy and `failsIf` are not read.
- Only follow-up and treatment strategies contribute costs and utilities to the nodes of the tree; detection strategies are used by the newborn screening model (see "Newborn screening").
- The DES (`DESStrategyPayoffs`) applies the strategy replacements by type with the same rules. The payoffs of the treatment and follow-up strategies of each arm (own costs, costs of their health technologies and utilities; all of them, not only the first one) are added to those of their health condition, with its schedule: for the disease, annual payoffs from time 0 and one-time payoffs at time 0 (D09); for a chronic manifestation or stage, annual payoffs while the patient has it and one-time payoffs at its onset (D11); for an acute manifestation, one-time payoffs with each episode, and annual payoffs are ignored with a warning (D10). The DES does not simulate the outcomes of the strategies: treatment and follow-up strategies with outcomes (`hasStrategyOutcome`, `failsIf`; D14), adverse events or guidelines (of the strategy or of its health technologies; D15) are rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`. The replacements of the strategies of a development are ignored with a warning.

## jades-hta: Strategy outcomes in decision trees

A strategy has mutually exclusive outcomes (decision D4 of `docs/osdi-1.3-diseno.md`): `StrategyOutcome`, with the subclasses `TreatmentOutcome` (`SuccessOutcome`, `FailureOutcome`) and `DetectionOutcome` (positive, negative and inconclusive results, which declare a risk for diseased and non-diseased individuals; supported in newborn screening models, see "Newborn screening"). `failsIf`, with `followedOnFailureBy`, `followedOnSuccessBy` and `resolvesOnSuccess`, is the short form of a failure and a default success (a failure produced by a health condition happens when the condition appears, at MAX(start of the strategy, onset age of the condition - initial age), P61); its failure condition is a parameter or a health condition (D5). The risk of an outcome (`hasOutcomeRisk`) is a probability per episode or a time to event (D6). The payoffs of an outcome are one-time (D7).

A relapse after the response (decision D9) needs no specific vocabulary: the success of a treatment is followed (`followedOnSuccessBy`, or the `followedByStrategy` of its `SuccessOutcome`) by a follow-up strategy (`FollowUpStrategy`) whose failure is the relapse, with the probability of relapse among the patients who responded, and which may be followed by the treatment of the relapse (P50). A long-term recurrence is a new episode of the same acute manifestation, which decision trees do not model yet (they count a single episode).

`StrategyOutcomeExpander` expands the outcomes below each leaf of the sub-tree of the disease, for every strategy with outcomes of the health conditions of the path (the outcomes are independent of the rest of branches):

- Each outcome is a branch with its risk; the outcome without risk takes the remaining probability. With `failsIf` a health condition, the failure is the only branch where the health condition is active, and the success, where it is not (P45).
- The outcome happens at T = start of the strategy + `hasEvaluationTime` (the start, with a warning, if missing). The annual payoffs of the strategy apply until T if the outcome is followed by another strategy or resolves the health condition, and until the end of the node otherwise (P42). The next strategy starts at T, and its own outcomes are expanded in turn.
- The payoffs of the outcome apply once, at T (annual ones are ignored with a warning); its effects apply to the leaf (P43, P46).
- An outcome that resolves the health condition stops its annual payoffs at T (P44); for an acute manifestation, nothing changes. Its effects on the life expectancy cannot stop in a decision tree, so they are kept, with a warning.
- The windows are relative to the node of the health condition (`PayoffWrapper.applyDiscount`).

Limitations:

- The next strategy of an outcome (or of the short form, or of the strategy) may be a group of alternative strategies (`Group` with `hasGroupItem`): each patient receives one of its strategies, with the proportion that the strategy declares (`hasProportionWithinGroup`); at most one strategy may lack it, taking the rest. Each strategy is a branch (P58). A group without proportions is not supported as the next strategy (N33), since in a strategy replacement a group means that all its strategies apply together; several strategies without proportion, or deterministic proportions that do not add up to 1 without a strategy taking the rest, are malformed (N34). Groups are not supported in the chains of detection strategies of newborn screening models.
- Rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`: cycles in the chain of strategies (N23; a repetition is another strategy), outcomes whose risk is a time to event without evaluation time or time horizon (N24), and strategies with outcomes whose costs follow a guideline. Rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`: more than one outcome without risk (N25), and the short form combined with explicit outcomes (N26).
- A failure produced by a health condition happens at the start of the strategy: the onset age of the health condition is not considered.
- Detection outcomes are only supported in the detection arms of newborn screening models (see "Newborn screening"); elsewhere, a detection strategy with explicit outcomes is rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`.
- The DES does not simulate the outcomes of the strategies: a treatment or follow-up strategy with outcomes is rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException` (D14).

## jades-hta: Adverse events in decision trees

An adverse event (`AdverseEvent`, decision D8 of `docs/osdi-1.3-diseno.md`) is a health condition caused by a strategy (`hasAdverseEvent`). Rule for the modeller: if the problem belongs to the natural history of the disease and the strategy only changes its risk, use an `Effect` of the strategy on that risk; if the problem is caused by the strategy, use an `AdverseEvent`.

In decision trees (`StrategyOutcomeExpander`), the adverse events of a strategy are expanded below the leaves each time a branch applies the strategy: the strategies of the health conditions of the path (with or without outcomes, after the replacements of the arm), the strategies that follow an outcome, and the strategies of an adverse event.

- Each adverse event is an independent branch with its risk (`hasRiskCharacterization`, mandatory: a probability per application of the strategy), and a branch without it. They are expanded before the outcomes of the strategy, which are independent of them, unless the strategy fails because of the adverse event (`failsIf` the adverse event): then the failure happens in the branches where the adverse event happened, and the success in the rest (P48).
- Its costs and utilities apply once, at the start of the strategy (D7); annual ones are ignored with a warning. Its effects apply to the leaf. An `InstantDeathEffect` splits the branch into death (with the probability of the magnitude of the effect) and survival; nothing else is expanded after the death, and the life expectancy of the leaf is 0, even if the strategy starts later (with a warning) (P49).
- The strategies of the adverse event (`hasStrategy`) are applied in its branch, with its own adverse events and outcomes.
- `isOccurrenceOf` only identifies the health condition: the adverse event does not activate its progression, payoffs or effects.
- Rejected: an adverse event without risk, with several risks or whose risk is not a parameter (`MalformedOSDiModelException`, N31); a risk that is a time to event (`UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`, N32, as the risks of the outcomes in D6); cycles in the chain of strategies; adverse events of the detection strategies of newborn screening models (`UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`).
- The DES does not simulate adverse events: a treatment or follow-up strategy with adverse events is rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`.

## jades-hta: Newborn screening

Detection interventions do not declare `hasSensitivity`, `hasSpecificity`, `detects` or `hasTemporaryThreshold` (`DetectionInterventionWrapper` rejects them with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`, N20 and N22): the disease they detect is their target (`isInterventionOf`), and the rest are properties of their detection strategies. For a detection intervention, the target applies to the population at risk, not only to those who present it.

The detection strategies of each arm (`NBSDetectionArm`) are those of the disease after the strategy replacements of the intervention of the arm, by type. A screening intervention usually replaces them with a group of a screening strategy and a diagnosis strategy (`PBD.ttl`, B01-B06). An arm may have at most one screening strategy and one diagnosis strategy (`UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`); a screening strategy in the arm without screening (N21) or a detection strategy that detects another disease are rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`.

- Screening arm: the whole population pays the own costs of the intervention with the screening category plus the costs of the screening strategy (own costs, health technologies and guidelines; B02). The sensitivity and specificity are those of the screening strategy (1, with a warning, if missing). True and false positives pay the diagnosis: the own costs of the disease with the diagnosis category plus the costs of the diagnosis strategy of the arm.
- Arm without screening: the patients with the disease pay the diagnosis.
- The strategy replacements of the arm also apply to the sub-tree of the disease (B03).
- Detection outcomes (decision D6, `NBSScreeningInterventionBuilder`): the individuals with and without the disease branch over the results of the screening strategy, using `hasRiskIfDiseased` or `hasRiskIfNotDiseased` (the outcome without risk for a status takes the remaining probability). The short form (sensitivity and specificity) is expanded into a positive and a negative outcome, so it builds the same tree as before (B01-B03, B04). A positive result without its own next strategy is followed by the `followedByStrategy` of the screening strategy or, otherwise, by the diagnosis strategy of the arm. A result followed by a diagnosis strategy is a perfect confirmation: the individuals pay the own costs of the disease with the diagnosis category plus the costs of that strategy; those with the disease are true positives (sub-tree of the disease with the effects of the intervention), and those without it are discarded (B06). A result followed by another detection strategy (a repeated or second-tier test) pays its costs and branches again over its results, for the same status (B05). A negative result ends the detection (its next strategy is ignored, with a warning). The payoffs of an outcome apply once and its effects apply to its branch; annual payoffs and the evaluation time are ignored with a warning, since the screening happens at the start of the simulation.
- Rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`: more than one outcome without risk for a status (N27), a positive or inconclusive result followed by a strategy that is not a detection strategy (N28), an inconclusive result without next strategy, an outcome that is not a positive, negative or inconclusive detection outcome, and the short form combined with explicit outcomes. Rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`: cycles in the chain of tests (N29; a repetition is another strategy).
- The sensitivity and specificity of the diagnosis strategy are not used: the diagnosis is assumed to be perfect. TODO: diagnosis strategies with explicit outcomes are rejected with an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`, both in the arm (N30) and as the confirmation of a result.
- `hasOutcomeRisk` is not used by detection outcomes. Their risks are `hasRiskIfDiseased` and `hasRiskIfNotDiseased`.
- TODO: false negatives do not pay a diagnosis. What happens with them depends on the disease (later clinical diagnosis, or death without diagnosis, partly because of a false sense of security after screening), so it is pending (see `docs/osdi-1.3-diseno.md`).
- The DES (`OSDiDetectionIntervention`) reads the sensitivity and specificity of a detection intervention from the detection strategy of its arm (its replacements or, otherwise, those of the diseases), and does not apply the costs of the strategies. TODO: it rejects a detection strategy with explicit outcomes (`UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`).

## jades-hta: Onset and end ages in decision trees

The onset and end ages of a progression (`hasOnsetAge`, `hasEndAge`) are ages, not times since the start of the model. The decision tree generator (`PayoffWrapper.applyDiscountToFormula`) translates them into times by subtracting the initial age of the population (`hasAge`; 0 if the population does not define it, with a warning). Only what happens between the initial age of the population and death (the undiscounted life years of each leaf) is accounted:

- Annual costs and disutilities are accounted from `MAX(0, onset age - initial age)` to `MIN(life years, end age - initial age)`. If the window starts after death, nothing is accounted.
- A one-time cost or disutility with an onset age is accounted at that age only if it happens between the initial age and death. A one-time payoff whose onset age is lower than the initial age is assumed to have happened before the start of the model, and it is not accounted.

## jades-hta: Effect magnitudes

The magnitude of a generic `Effect` is applied to each affected parameter or attribute according to the data item types of the magnitude and of the affected item (`EffectWrapper.resolveOperation`, decision D3 of `docs/osdi-1.3-diseno.md`):

| Magnitude | Operation | Rejected or warned |
|---|---|---|
| `DI_Factor`, `DI_Ratio` | `x * m` | — |
| `DI_RelativeRisk` | On a probability (probability, proportion, incidence, prevalence, birth prevalence, sensitivity, specificity): `1 - (1 - x)^m` (the rate equivalent to the probability is multiplied). On anything else: `x * m` | Warning if the item is not a probability (`DI_Factor` should be used). `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException` on a time to event (a hazard ratio must be used) |
| `DI_HazardRatio` | On a probability: `1 - (1 - x)^m`. On a time to event: `x / m` (exponential time) | Warning on a time to event; `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException` if the time has a distribution (first-order parameter); `MalformedOSDiModelException` on anything else |
| `DI_OddsRatio` | On a probability: `x * m / (1 - x + x * m)` | `MalformedOSDiModelException` on anything else |
| `DI_MeanDifference` | `x - m` | — |
| `DI_Continuous_Variable` and any other absolute type | `m` (replaces) | Warning if the type does not match that of the item, or if it is undefined (`DI_Undefined`, `DI_Other`) |
| `DI_StandardDeviation`, `DI_Lower95ConfidenceLimit`, `DI_Upper95ConfidenceLimit` | — | `MalformedOSDiModelException` |

A relative magnitude always modifies the item, even if both have the same type (e.g., a relative risk that modifies an increased mortality rate). The subclasses of `Effect` keep their own operation: instant death replaces, life expectancy reduction subtracts and increased mortality rate multiplies.

When several effects modify the same parameter (in a path of the decision tree, or in an intervention of the DES), they are applied in the order: replace, multiply (linear factors, then rate ratios, then odds ratios) and subtract (`EffectWrapper.sortForParameter`). More than one replacement of the same parameter is rejected with a `MalformedOSDiModelException`. A warning is logged if the multiplications use different scales.

Limitations:

- The rate conversion assumes a constant rate within the period of the probability. A relative risk is treated as a ratio of rates; to multiply a probability linearly (e.g., when the relative risk and the probability come from the same source and period), the magnitude must be a `DI_Factor`.
- Linear operations (`DI_Factor`, `DI_Ratio`, `DI_MeanDifference`) on a probability may produce values out of [0, 1]. They are not truncated; a warning is logged when both the probability and the magnitude are deterministic.
- A risk that is a time to event T (`DI_TimeToEvent`, exponential time) is converted in decision trees into the probability that the event happens within a period, `1 - e^(-period/T)` (decision D6): for a clinical progression, the period is the time horizon H of the experiment (P53), or 1 year, with a warning, if the experiment does not declare it (P26); for a strategy outcome, its evaluation time or, if it has none, the time horizon (P54; rejected without both, N24). The formula of each branch computes it from the time modified by the effects of the path: a hazard ratio m gives `1 - e^(-m·period/T)`. The synthetic parameter `P_<time>` is not written in the Excel file. The risk of an adverse event cannot be a time to event (N32). Limitation: the tree does not know when the event happens, so its payoffs start at the start of its node.
- The DES only applies the effects whose cause is an intervention, and it does not support times to event as risks yet.

## jades-hta: Sex of the population in the DES

The sex parameter of a population (`hasSex`) represents the heterogeneity of the sex in the population (first-order uncertainty), with 0 for men and 1 for women (`OSDiPopulation.initializeSexVariate`):

- A deterministic, second-order or calculated parameter is the proportion of women, p, and each patient's sex is drawn from a Bernoulli(p). A second-order parameter is the uncertainty on the true proportion: it is sampled once per simulation (its expected value in the base case), and the value is then the parameter of the Bernoulli. A deterministic value outside [0, 1] is rejected (`MalformedOSDiModelException`); a sampled value outside [0, 1] is not checked in advance.
- A first-order parameter must be a discrete distribution (e.g., Bernoulli), which is sampled for each patient; any other distribution is rejected (`MalformedOSDiModelException`).
- TODO: the age (`hasAge`) is still converted directly into a distribution that is sampled for each patient, so a second-order age is treated as first-order heterogeneity.

## jades-hta: Conformance between the decision tree and the DES

OSDi defines the semantics of a model; the decision tree generator (`osdi.decisiontree`) and the discrete-event simulation (`osdi.des`) implement it to a different extent. The table describes what each one does today. When a feature is not supported, the generator either rejects the model (`UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException`), logs a warning, or ignores it silently.

| OSDi feature | Decision tree | DES |
|---|---|---|
| Developments and their payoffs | Each development is a node of the tree (a chance node, the default branch of a group of alternatives, or an always active branch). Its costs (including those of its follow-up and treatment strategies), utilities and effects apply to every leaf of the sub-tree that hangs from it. | Built as a `Development` with only a name and a description: it never happens, and its costs, utilities and effects are not applied. `DiseaseFactory` logs a warning ("ignored by the DES") for each development with costs, strategies, utilities or effects. |
| Coexistent rules (`CoexistentCombinationRule`) | Processed as progressions without rule: an independent chance node per progression. | Ignored; `DiseaseFactory` logs a warning ("ignored by the DES") for each rule. Since every manifestation has its own independent pathway, the result for manifestations is the same as without rule. |
| Alternative rules (`AlternativeCombinationRule`, two or more members) | A single chance node with a branch per member. The member without risk takes the remaining probability (1 minus the sum of the rest); `hasNullProgression` adds a branch with no progression. Two members without risk, or a member without risk plus a null progression, are rejected (`MalformedOSDiModelException`). | Ignored; `DiseaseFactory` logs a warning ("ignored by the DES") for each rule. Each member with risk has its own pathway, so the members are competing but not mutually exclusive (unless `supersedesClinicalProgression` says so), and the member without risk never happens. |
| Progression without risk (outside a group of two or more alternatives) | Always active in its context: no chance node, its node is the only branch of its parent (probability 1). A manifestation without risk that depends on a development is active whenever such development is active. | No pathway is created, so it never happens unless the population assigns it an initial proportion. `DiseaseFactory` logs a warning ("will never occur in the DES") for each manifestation with neither risk nor initial proportion. |
| `hasInitialProportion` | Not supported: `DiseaseSubTreeGenerator` throws an `UnsupportedOSDiFeatureException` (trees assume that every patient starts without progressions). | Only for non-acute manifestations. At the start, each patient starts with the manifestation with such probability (`Disease.getInitialStage`), removing those superseded by others. A patient that starts with it does not go through its risk; the rest can still develop it through its risk. |
| `supersedesClinicalProgression` | Ignored silently: never read by the decision tree generator. | When a chronic progression happens, the progressions it supersedes are removed from the state of the patient; events of progressions superseded by the current state are cancelled; and the initial state does not include superseded progressions. |
| Target of the interventions (`hasIntervention`, `isInterventionOf`) | Read in both directions. The compared interventions must have the same target; if it is a clinical progression element, the tree starts with the patients that present it and only includes what depends on it (see "Target of the interventions"). | Read in both directions, with the same checks. The intervention is active during the whole simulation; if the target is a manifestation, every patient presents it at time 0 (a chronic one in its state, an acute one as an episode), and the population cannot contradict it (see "Target of the interventions"). Developments, stages and manifestations of a development are rejected as targets. |
| Strategies and guidelines (`hasStrategy`, `StrategyReplacementEffect`, `usesHealthTechnology`, `hasGuideline`) | Follow-up and treatment strategies add their costs (own costs plus those of their health technologies, per application according to a guideline) and utilities to those of their element. Interventions replace strategies by type in their arm (see "Strategies and guidelines in decision trees"). Guidelines with sub-guidelines, condition expressions or application ages are rejected. | Strategies are replaced by type in each arm, as in the tree. Follow-up and treatment strategies add their costs (own costs plus those of their health technologies) and utilities to those of their element, with its schedule. Strategies with outcomes, adverse events or guidelines are rejected (see "Strategies and guidelines in decision trees"). |
| Detection (newborn screening) | Sensitivity, specificity and costs of the screening and diagnosis strategies of each arm (see "Newborn screening"). Detection properties of interventions are rejected. False negatives do not pay a diagnosis. | Sensitivity and specificity of the detection strategy of the arm of each detection intervention, after the replacement by type (1 if not defined); more than one strategy of the type in an arm is rejected. Detection properties of interventions are rejected. Detection strategies with explicit outcomes are rejected. Tests are not scheduled yet. |
| Adverse events (`AdverseEvent`, `hasAdverseEvent`, `isOccurrenceOf`) | Expanded below the leaves each time a strategy is applied: independent branches with their probability, one-time payoffs, effects (an instant death is a branch of its own) and strategies (see "Adverse events in decision trees"). Not supported in the detection strategies of newborn screening models. | Not simulated: treatment and follow-up strategies with adverse events are rejected. |
| Strategy outcomes (`StrategyOutcome`, `failsIf`) | Treatment outcomes expanded below the leaves, with windows for the payoffs of the strategies and resolutions (see "Strategy outcomes in decision trees"). Detection outcomes in the screening arm of newborn screening models (see "Newborn screening"). | Not simulated: strategies with outcomes are rejected. |
| `hasDuration` | Ignored silently (the property is never read). | Ignored silently (the property is never read). |
| `hasFrequency` | Only read for acute manifestations: the one-time payoffs of the acute manifestation (and of its strategies, outcomes and adverse events) are multiplied by the expected number of episodes; ignored with a warning if the acute manifestation is the target of the interventions. | Only read for an acute manifestation that is the target of the interventions: the rate of its episodes after the one at time 0. Ignored silently otherwise. |
| Acute manifestations | A single episode within the lifetime, whose probability is the risk (see "Acute manifestations in decision trees"). One-time payoffs apply once; annual payoffs are ignored with a warning. | Simulated as events: they are not added to the state of the patient (so they have no annual payoffs), their one-time payoffs apply at each occurrence, and they can happen again, since their next occurrence is scheduled after each event. They never get an initial proportion, and their probability of death is not loaded from OSDi. |
| Time horizon of the experiment (`hasTimeHorizon`) | Written, without rounding, in the `timeHorizon` named range of the Excel file: the undiscounted life years of each leaf are limited to it, and so are the payoffs and QALYs that depend on them (P51, P52). Lifetime if not defined (the template keeps 150). | Used to limit the simulation (100 years if not defined), and it can be overridden from the command line. |
| Combination methods (`hasCostCombinationMethod`, `hasDisutilityCombinationMethod`, `hasLifeExpectancyReductionCombinationMethod`, `hasMortalityRateCombinationMethod`) | Costs: `ADD`, `ADD_DISTINCT`. Disutilities: `ADD`, `ADD_DISTINCT`, `MAX`, only among annual disutilities (one-time disutilities are always added), and `MAX` is applied to the discounted lifetime totals. Life expectancy reductions: `ADD`, `ADD_DISTINCT`, `MAX`. Mortality rates: `MAX`, `MULT`. The life expectancy of a leaf is the base life expectancy divided by the combined increased mortality rate, minus the combined reduction. | The same four methods and values, applied to the progressions active at each moment of the simulation (`Disease.getCostWithinPeriod`, `Patient.getUtilityValue`, death submodels), so `MAX` is applied period by period. |
| Effect magnitudes (`hasEffectMagnitude`) | Applied in the Excel formula of each parameter, with the operation given by the data item types (see "Effect magnitudes"); all the effects of the path, in the order replace, multiply, subtract. A time to event used as a risk becomes `1 - e^(-period/T)` (period: time horizon, evaluation time of an outcome or 1 year), with T modified by the effects. | Applied by a `CompositeParameterModifier` per parameter and intervention, with the same operations and order. Only effects caused by an intervention. |

## jades-hta: Future work: developments in the DES

The DES does not simulate developments or combination rules yet. A possible correspondence with the semantics already implemented in the decision trees:

- A group of alternatives is a categorical draw at the start of the simulation.
- A coexistent development with risk `p` is an independent draw with probability `p`.
- A development without risk is always present.
- The payoffs of a development are annual payoffs while the patient has it.
- The risk of a manifestation of a development only applies to patients with such development.
- A manifestation without risk has an initial proportion of 1 in its context.

## jades-hta: Update of costs in decision trees

The Excel file updates each cost to the year of the study with the CPI table of the template (`ROUND(1000*CPI(study)/CPI(year), 0)/1000`). A cost of the year of the study is not updated, since its factor is 1 (`ParamSheet.updateCost`). The CPI table of the template is the Spanish one, from 2002 to 2026: a study of another year can only be built if all its costs are of that year (P57, costs in Canadian dollars of 1997), and updating costs in another currency is only approximate.
